# Logistics Services LBO - Case Summary

## Investment Thesis
Asset-light logistics provider trading at 6.5x EBITDA. Fragmented market with consolidation runway. Strong pricing power + operational leverage through headcount productivity. PE can drive EBITDA from £30m → £52.5m through organic growth (5%), pricing (+3%), procurement efficiency, and headcount optimization.

## Entry Valuation
- Entry EBITDA: £30.0m
- Entry Multiple: 6.5x
- Entry EV: £195m
- Debt: £150m
- Equity: £145m

## Exit Assumptions
- Exit EBITDA: £52.5m (15% CAGR growth - more conservative than SaaS)
- Exit Multiple: 8.5x (multiple expansion on improving margins)
- Exit EV: £445.5m

## Value Creation Waterfall
| Lever | EBITDA Impact | % |
|---|---|---|
| Organic Growth | +£10m | 32% |
| Pricing Optimization | +£4m | 13% |
| COGS Efficiency (Procurement) | +£5m | 32% |
| OpEx Leverage (Headcount) | +£3.5m | 11% |
| Multiple Expansion | Portfolio | 12% |
| **Total Uplift** | **+£22.5m (75%)** | **100%** |

## Returns
- MoIC: 2.3x
- IRR: 20.8%

## Key Operational Levers
1. **Pricing:** +3% price increase on routes with low elasticity → +£4m EBITDA
2. **Procurement:** Vendor consolidation, fuel hedging → +£5m EBITDA
3. **Headcount:** Productivity from £200k to £250k per FTE → +£3.5m EBITDA
4. **Growth:** Organic 5% on expanded network → +£10m EBITDA

## Key Risks
- Fuel price volatility (mitigate: hedging program)
- Driver retention/turnover (mitigate: wage optimization)
- Customer concentration (mitigate: diversification)
- Competitive pricing pressure (mitigate: service differentiation)

## Comparison to SaaS
- **Growth:** Logistics slower (5% vs 30% CAGR)
- **Margins:** Logistics lower (20% vs 77% gross margin)
- **Returns:** Logistics lower (20.8% IRR vs 22.5% for SaaS)
- **Strategy:** Logistics is operational leverage play; SaaS is growth play
